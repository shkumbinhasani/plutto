# Plutto: technical notes

How the fonts are drawn, built, hinted and tested. The [README](../README.md) gives the overview.

Plutto is a narrow, plain sans for small sizes. Code in `plutto/` draws every outline. Its x-height is 540 units, caps 712, ascenders 775 and descenders −210, on a 1000-unit em.

## Build

```
python3 -m plutto
```

This needs Python 3, fontTools and numpy, plus brotli for the web files, skia-pathops for overlap removal and ttfautohint for hinting. `python3 -m plutto <folder>` writes to another folder. A build takes about three minutes. It writes these files to `fonts/` and prints their paths:

- `Plutto-Light`, `Plutto-Regular`, `Plutto-Medium`, `Plutto-SemiBold`, `Plutto-Bold`, `Plutto-ExtraBold` and `Plutto-Black`, as TTF and WOFF2.
- `Plutto-LightItalic`, `Plutto-Italic`, `Plutto-MediumItalic`, `Plutto-SemiBoldItalic`, `Plutto-BoldItalic`, `Plutto-ExtraBoldItalic` and `Plutto-BlackItalic`, as TTF and WOFF2.
- `Plutto-Variable` and `Plutto-Italic-Variable`, as TTF and WOFF2.
- `unhinted/`, the fourteen statics before hinting.

Light, Regular, Bold and Black (and their italics) are the drawn masters. Medium (500), SemiBold (600) and ExtraBold (800) are cut from the variable font of their design space with the fontTools instancer, so their outlines, advances and kerning are the variable font's at that weight. They get the static names, weight class, PANOSE weight, style bits and STAT of their own weight, then go through the same overlap removal and hinting as the masters. ttfautohint hints them with the shared settings (see Hinting). With those alone, small text at 11 to 16 px joined marks to their letters in about twenty places (à ŕ ẁ ý ỳ ŷ at SemiBold 11 px, ĉ š ĝ ĵ ŷ Ĩ at Medium Italic 11 px, ğ ĝ ĵ Ỉ at SemiBold Italic) and the i and j dots to their stems at SemiBold 12 px, so `hinting.py` knows the four cut styles by weight and italic bit and lifts those marks a pixel through `LIFTED_MARKS`, as it does for the masters. After that the tests find no joined mark or dot in any of them; the å ring closes at Medium 12 px, as it does at Regular 12 to 14 px. Because their kerning and advances are rounded separately after interpolation, a letter typed with combining marks can come out one unit wider or narrower than the precomposed letter in Medium and SemiBold, as in the variable fonts between masters.

Like Light and Black, Medium, SemiBold and ExtraBold are RIBBI families of their own: "Plutto Medium", "Plutto SemiBold" and "Plutto ExtraBold", each with a Regular and an Italic, with the typographic family "Plutto" and subfamilies such as "Medium Italic" and "ExtraBold".

Every static carries a full PANOSE classification: Latin text (2), normal sans serif (11), the weight from 3 (Light) to 10 (Black), even width (4; Plutto's capitals average 95% of Arial's width relative to the cap height, with O and H close in width), no contrast (2) and so no stroke variation (2), straight arms with horizontal terminals (2), normal contact letterforms (2) in the roman and oblique contact (9) in the italic, a standard trimmed midline (2, the A apex is cut flat) and a large constant x-height (4; 540 against a 712 cap height).

The variable fonts are built from the drawn masters, with overlapping contours. The statics then have their overlaps removed (fontTools `removeOverlaps` with skia-pathops), so every renderer and printer sees one clean outline per shape; points that the merge leaves twice in a row are folded together without changing the curve. The merge also moves some points a unit or two off the line they were drawn on, such as the Bold f bar top to 542 instead of 540. After it, `compile.py` puts every point within two units of the baseline, x-height, cap height, ascender, descender or one of their overshoot lines back on that line, but only where the drawn outline had that line at that place: a flat edge on the line that spans the point, or a drawn point on the line within two units sideways. Points that were never on a line, such as the i dot top at 714, stay where they are. Without skia-pathops the build says so on stderr and ships the statics with overlaps. Every file carries a `meta` table that names its scripts (Latin, Greek, Cyrillic).

Glyphs whose outline is another glyph or glyphs at an offset ship as TrueType composites (`composed` in `compile.py`): accented letters (base plus marks), letters another script reuses (Cyrillic а is Latin a), the tabular figures (the proportional figure moved to the centre of the tabular width), and repeats such as " … · and „. After drawing, the build compares the compiled contours of every glyph with every other glyph's: a glyph becomes a composite when its contours, in order, are those of other glyphs each moved by one offset, in every master of its design space, so the variable fonts carry the offsets as deltas. In the roman the match must be exact. In the italic, where the narrowing leaves marks at fractional offsets, a point may differ by one unit and the offset is rounded, but only for a letter with one mark; spacing accents and letters with two marks must match exactly there, since two rounded offsets drift from where the `mark` feature puts the same marks. Components are always simple glyphs, so composites are one level deep, and a glyph that copies another uses it as a component only if the other comes earlier in the glyph order. Two groups stay outlines: the letters that `hinting.py` lifts by point index (`LIFTED_MARKS`), and the letters it hints better whole (`HINTED_WHOLE`: å Å ĥ ȟ ǩ ĩ ỉ ĺ ỷ, whose marks hinting joined to the letter at single sizes from 11 to 16 px once they were composites). Of 1,741 glyphs, 716 are composites in the roman statics and 537 in the italic ones, ď ľ Ľ ť among them, so fontbakery can check their caron; the variable fonts carry 753 and 547. When the components of a composite overlap, as the cedilla or ogonek of ç and ą do, the variable fonts keep the composite and flag the overlap on its first component (`OVERLAP_COMPOUND`), and `removeOverlaps` decomposes and merges it in the statics. The hinted statics shrank by 24 to 26% in the roman (Regular from 410 to 305 KB) and 14 to 16% in the italic, their WOFF2 files by 13 to 15% and 6 to 9%, the variable fonts by 22% (635 to 497 KB) and 16% (787 to 664 KB), and the unhinted statics by 7%; all the files together went from 14.6 to 12.5 MB.

Kerning is generated as class pairs plus many single pairs: the italic drift corrections, the floors and the clearance passes each add pairs for every letter with an accent. The build folds them before compiling (`folded_texts` in `features.py`). It does this for all masters of a design space together, so the variable fonts get the same lookups and classes in every master. In each pair lookup it keeps the class rules, takes out the single pairs, and adds a lookup right after it with the difference between each single pair and what the class rules give. Lookups in one feature add up, so the totals are unchanged. That lookup is a class matrix: left glyphs whose rows of differences agree within a unit share a class, as do right glyphs with matching columns, and a merged cell takes the loosest value, so no clearance gets tighter; differences of one unit or less are dropped. Where a matrix would be larger than the single pairs, the singles stay. Contextual kerning (the rules that kern across combining marks) is folded as well: two rules that call the same lookup and differ in one glyph position become one rule with both glyphs there, unless a rule between them could match the same glyphs. Every pair renders within one unit of the unfolded kerning. In the Italic the single pairs went from 30,611 to 7,556 and the contextual rules from 2,546 to 594; GPOS shrank from 260 to 150 KB in the Italic and from 166 to 110 KB in the Regular, and the variable fonts' from 243 and 344 KB to 189 and 240 KB.

Glyph names longer than 31 characters (twelve polytonic Greek letters such as `alphapsiliperispomeniypogegrammeni`) are renamed to their production names (`uni1F86`) in every built font (`production_name` in `compile.py`). The build reopens each file and renames the glyph order before any table is read back, so `cmap`, `GSUB`, `GPOS` and `GDEF`, which store glyph numbers, stay as they are; only `post` changes. Feature code and tests keep the long names in the source.

Vertical metrics follow the Google Fonts guide: the typo and hhea ascender equal the top of Ắ (U+1EAE) in the tallest master, 994 at Black, so stacked Vietnamese capitals stay inside the line. Under a tilde (Ẫ Ễ Ỗ) the circumflex is the stack's flattest mark, so it keeps more of its height there (`HAT_PRESS_HEIGHT` in `accents.py`, 113 units at Bold and 109 at Black against 148 and 160 alone) and the tilde above it is shallower (`HAT_TONE_HEIGHT`), with the 22-unit gap between them unchanged, so the stack still ends under 994; the descender stays at −250 and both line gaps at 0, with `USE_TYPO_METRICS` set. The build measures Ắ from the drawn outlines, so the value follows changes to the marks or the cap height, and never drops below the 950 of `LINE_ASCENT`. The win values cover the tallest and deepest point of the family, as before.

## Hinting

The build saves each static to `unhinted/`, runs ttfautohint on it and writes the hinted file to `fonts/`; the WOFF2 files are made from the hinted TTFs. A rebuild deletes any `Plutto-*` font in the folder that it no longer writes, and says so on stderr. `hinting.py` holds the settings, the same for all fourteen statics:

- `--stem-width-mode=nsn`: natural stems in grayscale and DirectWrite, strong stems only for GDI ClearType. FreeType follows the third letter, the DirectWrite one. The default, `qsq`, and `nsq` round stems to whole pixels there; on these fonts that joins more marks to their letters at 11 to 16 px (Black 24 against 2, Bold 10 against 7) and fills in more counters (Black 7 against 4, Black Italic 10 against 5).
- `--x-height-snapping-exceptions=11,13,15,17,19,21,23-24`: at these sizes ttfautohint rounds the x-height but does not scale the whole glyph to fit it. With the 775 ascender, caps, ascenders and x-height land on three different pixel rows at every size from 11 to 24 px, and no cap or ascender moves more than 0.70 px from its drawn height. At 11 px the exception lets the ascender round up, which also keeps the marks over x-height letters clear of them.
- `--increase-x-height=0`: ttfautohint's default rounds the x-height up more often at 6 to 14 px. On these fonts that only changes 6 px, where the x-height becomes 4 px instead of 3; the x-height is already 54% of the em, so the build turns it off.
- `--windows-compatibility`: blue zones for usWinAscent and usWinDescent, so Windows does not clip.
- `--no-info`: the version string stays as built.

At Bold and Black the hinter fills some counters at small sizes. For Bold, Bold Italic, Black and Black Italic, `hinting.py` writes a ttfautohint control file from `OPENED_COUNTERS`: at the sizes listed for a glyph, the points above each counter move up half a pixel and the points below it move down half a pixel. It finds the counters from the outline, so counters made where contours overlap count too, such as the A crossbar, the e eye in æ and œ, and both counters of &. Points on the baseline, x-height, cap height, ascender and descender do not move, and neither do counters that start above the x-height, such as the å ring. A glyph that contains a listed glyph's contours unchanged gets the same moves, so listing a covers á, à, â, ä, ã and å. The same file lifts the marks of a few accented letters by a pixel at single sizes where hinting rounds them onto their letter (`LIFTED_MARKS`, such as ĝ ĵ ŷ at Bold 16 px, ή at Bold 16 px and ў at Regular 16 px); their drawn gap matches letters that render clean, such as ê and î. The dot over the ascender of ḃ ḋ ḟ lifts at 16 px in every style, and the dot of ṫ at 11 px from Regular up (and ḟ's at 11 px at Black, Bold Italic and Black Italic), where hinting rounds the ascender or the t up into it. The style comes from the font's weight class and italic bit; the Medium, SemiBold and ExtraBold statics get their own `LIFTED_MARKS` entries, and ExtraBold and ExtraBold Italic open the æ and œ eyes at 11 px through `OPENED_COUNTERS`; any other weight between masters, such as an instance at 550, gets the shared settings and no control file.

ttfautohint hints a composite through its components, so the mark of a composite letter is hinted on its own, as a typed combining mark is. That was a pixel worse than hinting the letter whole for nine letters, which stay outlines (`HINTED_WHOLE`), and it moved the Å ring a pixel onto the A at 11 px in Medium, SemiBold, ExtraBold and SemiBold Italic once A's sidebearings tightened; `LIFTED_MARKS` lifts it there, as it already did in Regular, Bold and the other italics.

The hinted files also set bit 3 of `head.flags`, so the ppem rounds to a whole number. The hints change nothing else: outlines, metrics, names and layout tables match the unhinted file.

If ttfautohint is not on the PATH, the build copies the unhinted statics into place, says on stderr that it skipped hinting, and still writes every file.

The variable fonts stay unhinted. Like every file, they carry a `gasp` table that asks for smoothing at all sizes (0x000F), and a `prep` program that only turns on smart dropout control.

`hinting-check/joins.py` and `hinting-check/heights.py` measure small text with FreeType's v40 interpreter against the same file rendered with `FT_LOAD_NO_HINTING`. They need a throwaway environment with freetype-py, numpy, scipy, Pillow and fontTools:

```
python3 -m venv /tmp/ft && /tmp/ft/bin/pip install freetype-py numpy scipy pillow fonttools
/tmp/ft/bin/python hinting-check/joins.py fonts/unhinted --hinted fonts
```

`joins.py` checks 11 to 16 px unless `--sizes` names others, such as `--sizes 11-18`. The caron of ď ľ Ľ ť stands beside the stem rather than above the letter, so for those four it counts separate shapes instead of looking for a gap above the letter: hinting joins them where the hinted render has fewer shapes than the unhinted one.

What the tests find in hinted text from 11 to 16 px, rendered through Pillow:

- Marks: hinting joins no mark to its letter, in any style. Before the hinting and drawing work it joined 325, nearly all at Bold and Black.
- Dots: the i and j dots stand clear of the stem at every size and style.
- Counters: hinting fills in three counters that the unhinted render shows: the å ring at 13 and 14 px in Regular and at 11 px in Black. The ring is under 2 px wide there, and moving its points made it nearly vanish. Below 11 px, Black œ at 9 px, Black Italic æ at 10 px and Ø at 9 px, and Æ at 9 px in Regular, Light Italic and Italic still close.

The marks needed drawing changes as well as settings: no setting kept them apart while the Bold and Black dots and accents sat as close as they first did. At Bold and Black the i and j dots now sit 88 and 94 units above the x-height, accents keep their own clearance tiers in `accents.py`, and the marks on y lift a little at Bold and Black.

Regular, Italic, Bold and Bold Italic install as the family "Plutto". Light and Light Italic install as "Plutto Light", Black and Black Italic as "Plutto Black", Medium and SemiBold with their italics as "Plutto Medium" and "Plutto SemiBold", and apps that read typographic names group all fourteen under "Plutto". The italic statics set the italic bits in OS/2 and head, a post angle of −9° and a matching caret slope.

Each variable font has a weight axis from 300 to 900 with 400 as the default and named instances from Light to Black (Light Italic to Black Italic). Both install as "Plutto Variable", one as Regular and one as Italic. Their STAT tables add an `ital` axis: the roman font names value 0 "Roman" (elidable, linked to 1) and the italic font names value 1 "Italic", so apps can pair the two files.

Four masters drive each weight axis: Light 300, Regular 400, Bold 700 and Black 900. In code, `m.v(regular, bold)` extends in a straight line to Light and Black; `m.v(regular, bold, light=..., black=...)` sets the end values, and weights between masters interpolate piecewise.

## Italic

The italic is a true italic for text and interfaces: it slants 9°, sets 3.5% narrower than the roman and draws its own forms for the letters where an italic reads differently, the way Inter 4's italic (single-storey a, cursive e, f with a descender) and IBM Plex Sans, Source Sans and Fira Sans Italic do. It stays restrained: the stems, terminals and curves are Plutto's, and the weight matches the roman.

Forms drawn for the italic (the `italic_` functions at the end of `lowercase.py`, the Cyrillic forms at the end of `cyrillic.py`, and `italics.py` for glyphs built in other modules):

- a is single-storey: the d bowl with a stem that ends in an exit stroke, the same hook that ends Plutto's l and t. Every accented a and Cyrillic а, ӑ and ӓ follow it. ª, æ, ǽ and @ use the single-storey a without the exit stroke: in æ the e takes its place, joining the a's stem with its round left side inside the stem, so the join is no wider than a stem, and @ wraps its tail around the a's stem as the roman does. `ss01` changes nothing in the italic.
- d and u end their right stem in the same exit stroke; ď, đ, ₫, ú, ų, и and the rest follow. đ and ₫ measure their bars from the stem, not from the tail (`italics.py`). ц keeps an upright post, which its descender needs.
- n, m and h have asymmetric shoulders: the arch leaves the stem about 40 units lower than in the roman and peaks at 0.6 of the width instead of 0.55, as a pen does when it springs from the stem. Their right stems stay straight, so n and u stay distinct at 11 px. u is the turned arch, so its bowl leans the same way; Cyrillic п, т, ш and щ follow n, m and the turned m.
- k, v, w, x and z follow the restrained pattern of true sans italics such as Gill Sans Italic and Seravek Italic: they keep their roman structure and draw one stroke as a curve, as the y tail does. k curves its upper arm into the stem and starts the straight leg from a knee on that arm, as the roman k does (`curved_kay` in `lowercase.py`); ķ, ǩ, ĸ, Cyrillic к and ќ and Greek κ use the same arm. v and w bow their last stroke outward and keep a pointed vertex; x bows its back stroke; z bows its diagonal. The inner edge of each bowed stroke is a parallel of the outer edge, offset square to the curve, so the stroke keeps its roman weight along its length; z's diagonal is drawn 4% heavier at Bold and 7% at Black, where the italic correction thins a curved diagonal by that much. Accented letters and Cyrillic х follow; қ, ҡ, ҝ and ж have their own drawings and keep straight arms.
- κ curves its upper arm into the stem (`italic_kappa` in `italics.py`). Greek is a cursive script already, and Plutto's α, ι, τ, υ and ω already have italic shapes; κ was the one letter drawn like a Latin k. Designers asked about Greek italics name θ, β, κ and φ: Gerry Leonidas suggested the rounded β and the cursive θ for ClearType italics, Christian Thalmann redrew θ, κ and φ. Plutto's β, θ and φ are already round, and the cursive θ reads as ϑ, a separate character used in mathematics, so they keep their forms.
- e has a bar that rises about 6° and turns into the bowl through round joins inside and out, instead of meeting it at a corner. é, ê, ë, ę, ё, ə (a turned e) and the rest follow.
- f runs down to the descender and ends in a hook like the j's. ﬁ and ﬂ use it.
- y ends in a curved tail that turns left under the letter. ý, ÿ, ỳ, у, ў and the rest follow.
- Cyrillic и, й, ѝ, ӣ and ӥ take the u shape, п the n shape, т the m shape, and ц, ш and щ the round forms that Bulgarian text uses in the roman. д takes the ∂ form of Russian italics: Plutto's ð without its bar, a round bowl with a stroke that rises to the ascender and leans left. Bulgarian, Serbian and Macedonian text keeps the g-shaped д through `locl`, since the ∂ form is not used there. г and в keep their shapes: Russian sans italics keep them, the г built on ı is a Serbian form, and the round в that rises to the ascender is the Bulgarian one.
- Serbian and Macedonian text (`locl` for SRB and MKD) gets the local italic forms: г as ı with a bar, д as g, п and т as n and m with a bar, and the Serbian б the roman already has. The substitution runs in both fonts so every glyph stays reachable, but the roman forms draw like the default letters; only the italic shows the change.

`ItalicForm` in `oblique.py` pairs a roman drawing with an italic one and draws whichever the master asks for. `lowercase.py` and `cyrillic.py` rebind the letters at their end, so everything that builds on a letter (accented letters through `accents.py`, the mark anchors measured from the upright drawing, the Cyrillic and Latin letters built from it) gets the italic form in the italic masters. Every italic master runs the same drawing, so the four italic masters stay compatible.

`masters.py` gives every master an `italic` flag, a `slant` and a `narrowing` (`ITALIC_WIDTH`, 0.965 for the italic, 1 for the roman). Glyph code draws upright; `oblique.py` maps each drawing to x′ = 0.965·x + tan 9°·(y − x-height/2) in two passes, first narrowing it upright and then slanting it, and after each pass corrects it against the drawing it started from:

- Curves take their handle lengths from a similarity of the chord rather than from the map, so the acute and obtuse corners of a slanted bowl round off alike instead of leaning.
- Stroke weight is matched to the upright drawing, stroke by stroke. Rays across the stroke measure the thickness at each line and at the ends and middle of each curve, before and after the map; edges then move by half the difference. Straight lines use the square ray only. On curves the shortest of three rays counts, if it leaves through an edge that runs nearly parallel to the curve. Stems keep their weight although the narrowing would thin them by 3.5%, `/` diagonals stop thinning, `\` diagonals stop thickening and bowls stop growing heavy on the NE–SW diagonal.
- Edges hidden under other ink, terminal ends and rays that leave through a stroke end are left in place, and corners that two contours share move together.
- Corners on the baseline, x-height, cap height, ascender, descender or their overshoots, corners at the glyph's own top and bottom, and corners next to a flat or at a curve's vertical extreme keep their height and move only sideways.
- Joins that are smooth upright stay smooth.
- Curve handles shorter than 3 units grow to 3 along their direction, so no control point rounds onto its end point when the font is built.
- An edge counts as hidden under other ink when a point 3 units along it and half a unit outside it is inked. Probing a unit outside, as before, landed in the other arm of sharp notches such as the inside of the Black V, and left them uncorrected.

`slanted` returns the corrected outline before the narrowing, so accents, slashes and other parts laid over a letter (`oblique.Overlaid`) are placed with the upright measures; `finished` then narrows the whole glyph, marks included, and `glyphs.render` uses it. Mark anchors and the `<x 0 advance 0>` records that make room for a caron or a Greek mark are measured on the upright drawing too, so `features.py` moves them by the same map, and a letter typed with combining marks matches the precomposed one.

Some glyphs are built differently:

- Arrows, math signs (+ − × ÷ = ≠ < > ≤ ≥ ± ≈ ~ ^ ¬ ∞) and symbolic signs (° ¤ © ® ™ •) stay upright and unnarrowed (`UPRIGHT_IN_ITALIC` in `glyphs.py`, with their `.case` and `.sups` forms). Their advance narrows like every other, and they move sideways to sit where the narrowed, slanted centre falls. Punctuation, brackets, | ¦ † ‡ § ¶ # % and the middle dot slant with the text.
- The slashed zeros draw their slash after the slant: the plain zero slants and corrects as usual, then `alternates.slash_through` lays a slash across the slanted bowl, turned by the slant so it keeps its roman angle to the bowl's axis.
- The ø and Ø slashes slant with the letter. They keep their roman overhang above and below the bowl and stay within the bowl's sides.

Every advance is the roman advance times 0.965, apart from the glyphs with their own italic drawing, whose advance is their own drawing's times 0.965, and tabular glyphs. `figures.tabular` builds a tabular glyph 1/0.965 as wide in the italic masters and centres it, so after the narrowing it has exactly the roman tabular width in every italic static, Medium and SemiBold included; the figures themselves narrow like the proportional ones. Glyphs that `figures.tabular` builds, such as tabular punctuation and the figure space, follow.

Kerning: the italic class kerns start from the roman values times 0.965, then `features.py` measures the nearest horizontal gap of every member pair of each kerning class in the roman and in the italic of the same weight, on the italic's own shapes, and moves the class value by the mean change from 0.965 times the roman gap. Where one member drifts more than 5 units from its class mean, or its class value would leave its nearest gap more than 4 units off (5 less a unit for the two roundings of roman and italic kerns), in any italic master, it gets its own pair; its own value stays within 4 units of the nearest-gap reading. Letters with their own italic form (a, e, f, y and the glyphs built on them, see `glyphs.drawn_alike`) have no roman gap to match, since a different shape sets the nearest gap: against F the roman a's nearest ink is its stem, the italic a's is its bowl. They stay out of the class mean and kern with the class value, as the o, d and q they now resemble do. All other kerned italic pairs keep 0.965 of their roman gap. The rooms that `<x 0 advance 0>` records add are rounded against the base letter's narrowed advance, so a letter with a typed caron or Greek breathing is exactly as wide as the precomposed one. The clearance passes (accents, brackets and j, f and closing brackets, fractions, single-storey a) measure the italic outlines. The italic adds one more (`ITALIC_CLEARANCE` in `features.py`): f, ﬁ, ﬂ and j after an opening bracket, a slash, an opening or low quote or a left guillemet keep the o–o gap, since the italic f's descender reaches under the glyph before it. The roman f has no descender, and the roman kerning does not change. In both romans and italics, f before ' " ’ ” ‘ “ ? ! and * keeps the full o–o gap (`CLEARANCE`), where it keeps 0.7 of it before ) ] and }: the f's hook reaches over the mark at its own height.

The round capitals and figures (O C G Q S, 0 6 8 9) keep the corrected oblique. Plutto's rounds have no stress, so there is no axis to turn more cursively; the weight correction holds their stroke profile within 4% of the roman, and they sit evenly with the cursive lowercase. The Serbian locl forms kern toward the letter they replace (`TWINS` in `cyrillic.py`), never closer than the floor, so the bars on n̄ and m̄ do not run into a T.

## Combining marks

The fonts carry twenty-two combining marks: grave, acute, circumflex, tilde, macron, breve, dot above, dieresis, hook above, ring, double acute, caron, turned comma above, horn, dot below, diaeresis below, comma below, cedilla, ogonek, vertical line below, tilde below and macron below (U+0300 to U+030C, U+0312, U+031B, U+0323, U+0324, U+0326 to U+0329, U+0330, U+0331), plus the dotted circle ◌ that shapers show under a mark with no letter. A letter followed by one or more of them shapes the same as the precomposed letter where there is one, and sits at the same height where there is none, so ṕ or ḗ work without their own glyphs.

The precomposed letters are built from the same parts: `accents.py` lays the mark over the letter at the point the `mark` feature would use, so a letter typed in either form draws the same. Marks over an ascender (ĥ, ĺ) sit above it, centred on the stem. Ď, ď, Ľ, ľ and ť take a narrow wedge-shaped caron beside the stem, and the letter widens by the room the kerning gives a typed caron. The italic correction pulls the wedge toward the slanted stem, so the italic sets it further out (`CARON_ITALIC_GAP`): it clears the stem by 22 to 26 units in the Italic and 15 to 18 in the Black Italic, as far as in the roman of the same weight. The dot below sits under the letter, or beside a descender that runs through its place: ỵ puts it right of the tail, as Vietnamese typography guides advise, and `dotbelow_x` checks the whole height of the dot, not only its middle, once a descender is in the way.

- `ccmp` swaps i and j for their dotless forms before a mark above, picks the capital forms of the marks over capitals, picks narrow marks over i and I, and swaps the caron for a tick beside the stem after d, l and L, and for a shorter tick after t (`caroncomb.alt.short`), so ť clears its crossbar. After G, K, L, N, R, k, l, n and r it turns a cedilla into the comma below, and after g into the turned comma above, as Latvian writes Ģ, Ķ, Ļ, Ņ, Ŗ and ģ; Ş and Ţ keep the cedilla. It joins a circumflex and a following acute, grave, hook above or tilde into one mark, Vietnamese style: the acute, grave and hook sit beside the circumflex's right arm and the tilde above it. i and j drop their dot before a mark above even with one or two marks below in between, and į and ị split into dotless i and their mark first.
- `mark` hangs each mark on its letter from anchors measured on the outlines; `mkmk` stacks a second mark on the first.
- GDEF gives the marks their own class. The build sets it from the font: combining marks and the glyphs the mark lookups place are marks, and ligatures from GSUB are ligatures.
- Kerning carries over the marks. The `accent_room` lookups give a letter and its marks the same room against T, V, W, Y, F and f as the precomposed letter. The `accent_across` lookups apply the kern of the two letters across one or two marks, as in ĀV or Ťo. The same rules run in every master of a design space, so the variable fonts merge.

ttfautohint hints each mark glyph on its own, so at 12 px a hinted mark over a letter can sit a pixel higher than the same mark in the precomposed letter.

## Test

```
python3 -m unittest discover tests
```

The tests build the fonts into a temporary folder and check, among other things:

- that every printable ASCII character is mapped and every glyph has ink;
- that no outline traces a segment twice at any weight: between each pair of neighbouring masters, read from the variable fonts, the test solves for the weights where two segments, or a segment's two ends, come within a unit of each other;
- that no ink passes the line metrics, that the typo and hhea ascender cover Ắ in every font with both line gaps at 0, and that no glyph name is longer than 31 characters;
- that accented letters, ď ľ Ľ ť among them, are composites in the hinted, unhinted and variable fonts, that every component is a simple glyph, that the masters of a design space share their components, and that the hinted statics keep the components and offsets of `unhinted/`;
- that A, V, W and Y sit 8 to 12 units from their neighbours at Regular and T 13 to 17;
- that kerning and the OpenType features (kern, tnum, pnum, aalt, zero, case, frac, numr, dnom, sups, subs, sinf, ss01 to ss06, cv06 to cv08) change shaping as they should, at the fourteen statics and at weights 350, 550 and 800; that default shaping leaves -> 2x3 No. 1/2 and lowercase punctuation such as e-mail, (Hello) and a:b as typed, while `calt` raises A-B, 10:30, (ABC), [1], «ÉTÉ» and @NASA; that tnum reaches the tabular punctuation; that `locl` gives Ș Ț for Romanian and Moldavian but not Turkish, the Lithuanian dotted i under accents and the Dutch í j́; and that every stylistic set and character variant carries a name;
- that every static carries the full PANOSE classification, that within each font the tabular punctuation, figure space and punctuation space keep to the tabular figure width and its half, and that the romans of each family share one tabular width;
- that a letter typed as base and combining marks matches the precomposed letter in width and ink, with the precomposed letters dropped from a copy of the character map (a circumflex and a Vietnamese tone mark count as one glyph, since `ccmp` joins them); that marks with no precomposed letter (n̈, ḿ, Ĥ) sit as over their model letter; that stacked marks keep 20 units apart; and that GDEF classes follow the ligatures and marks;
- that key glyphs keep their counters at every roman master (at least 12% of Regular) and every italic master (at least 75% of the roman master of the same weight), and that apertures in a, e, s, S, $, § and others stay open when the ink spreads by 20 units in a round pen;
- that the italic files carry the right names, style bits, angle, caret slope and STAT links, that each variable font at a master weight draws the same ink as its overlap-free static (compared with a nonzero-winding rasterizer, allowing a one-pixel band along the edges), and that italic advances are the roman's narrowed by `ITALIC_WIDTH` (0.965), with tabular figures still sharing one width;
- that italic stems and diagonals keep their roman weight within 2%, short strokes included, that round letters keep their roman stroke profile within 4% (joined ones such as a, g, 8, ð, æ, œ and & within 4% of the nearest roman reading), that contours meet at their roman angle, and that upright glyphs only move sideways;
- that italic corners on the vertical metrics keep their height, round letters keep their overshoot, joins smooth in the roman stay smooth where they show (joins hidden under other ink may turn), slashed zeros put no ink outside their bowl, ø and Ø stay within o and O sideways, and kerned italic pairs keep their roman gap within 6 units.

They also check that every file the build writes loads and is one the build meant to write, that a rebuild finds old `Plutto-*` files to delete and leaves other files alone, that the hinted statics keep the outlines, metrics and names of `unhinted/` and only add instructions, that the WOFF2 files keep the hints, and that the variable fonts and `unhinted/` carry no hints. For hinted text from 11 to 16 px, rendered with Pillow against the same font with its glyph programs removed (Pillow would otherwise run FreeType's autohinter on the unhinted file), they check:

- one x-height row and one baseline row, and ink within 8% of the unhinted text;
- that x, H, d and E reach the same pixel row in all fourteen styles from 11 to 24 px;
- that hinting joins no mark to its letter in i, j and the accented letters where the unhinted render keeps a clean gap;
- that the i and j dots stand clear at every size;
- that no counter in the a family, e, g, o, ø, 6, 8, 9, 0, 4, A, B, P, R, &, @, æ and œ that the unhinted render shows is filled in, apart from the three å ring cases in `KNOWN_CLOSED_COUNTERS`;
- that a font at a weight between masters hints without the per-style control file.

The tests read the twelve long Greek glyph names under their production names in the built fonts (`built_name` in `tests/support.py`). Kerning that eases a mark at a weight between masters may come one unit closer than at the masters (`INTERPOLATED_SLACK`), since the kerns interpolate in a straight line and the mark geometry does not; with T tight, T before î and ĵ at Medium came 4.3 units under the target against a 4-unit slack.

They also check that overlap removal leaves no point within two units of a metric line where the drawn outline had that line, that every static's GPOS stays under 181 KB, that LY, 7. 7, and 7: are kerned, and that the italic д rises to the ascender while its Serbian form keeps the descender.

The suite runs 131 tests in about eleven minutes. They run without HarfBuzz or ttfautohint, but some shaping and hinting checks skip unless `hb-shape` and `ttfautohint` are on the path. Set `PLUTTO_REQUIRE_SHAPING=1` or `PLUTTO_REQUIRE_HINTING=1` to make a missing tool fail the run instead.

## Weight extremes: known weak glyphs

Light and Black have their own values wherever a straight line from Regular and Bold went wrong. Round capitals and figures overshoot by 14 units (`CAP_OV`), lowercase rounds by 10 (`OV`) and the n, m, h and u arches by 7 (`ARCH_OV`). Every letter adds an even opening to its sidebearings in `spaced` (`opening` in `parts.py`): capitals more than lowercase, so caps and text both breathe at small sizes. Bold and Black add a little even spacing (`track` in `spaced`: 2 units a side at Bold, 6 at Black) so heavy text keeps its rhythm, and their horizontals are heavier than a straight line gives: the H crossbar is 0.82 of the stem at Bold and 0.80 at Black. The guillemets are drawn as two parallel arms at about 55°, cut flat at top and bottom, so the notch stays as deep as the arm is long at every weight. In the italic the two marks of « and » sit further apart, by the amount the slant flattens the upper arms, and keep the upright's 58-unit gap; the advance stays the same. They kern in their own classes against T, V, W, Y, L and r instead of borrowing the hyphen's kerns, and the case forms do not kern, since at Black they reach within 5 units of the T bar. At Black, the æ, œ and æ.ss01 eyes keep the same counter as e, ¢ takes its bowl from c, and the 3 opens about as wide as the S.

The slashed zero draws its bowl a little lighter than the plain zero (0.97 at Bold, 0.93 at Black), so the slash does not make it darker than the other figures; beside the slash it keeps a third more counter at Black than before. Ŋ switches to the N-shaped form with a hooked right stem in Northern, Lule, Inari and Skolt Sami text (`locl`). ∑, ∏ and ∆ slant with the italic: kept upright, their corners ran into slanted neighbours.

Kerning keeps a floor under the pairs that came closest: r, v, w, x, y and ſ before t and Æ, the upright signs (¤ ™ © ® = ≠ ≈ ↑ →) after letters, and ± ≤ ≥ ∑ ∏ ∫ ∆ before them. No pair of letters, figures or signs used in text comes closer than 17 units at any weight. The tightest pairs left are ones no language writes: ł before î at 7 units, X before Ŧ at 8, ï before ™ and ¤ before 2 at −2 and −1, all in Black Italic.

What is still a compromise, and why:

- `lowercase.py`: the i and j dots stand 81 units above l at Black. The dot is already narrower than the stem there (182 against 204), and a smaller gap lets hinting join it to the stem at 11 px, so neither can shrink. RefSansA goes 84 over and RefSansB 38.
- `capitals.py`, `figures.py`: the Black A keeps about 0.3 of its Regular counter, B, P and R about 0.45, and the 4 about 0.32, all within the range of the reference fonts. W and M stay wide at Black (1.30 and 1.26 times H), also inside that range.
- `oblique.py` (italic): g at Black Italic is 3.6% off its roman stroke profile, the largest drift among joined letters; it does not show at text sizes.
- `latin.py`: Ħ, ħ, Ł, ł and đ kern like H, h, L, l and d although their bars reach a little past those letters; measured over every letter and figure, no pair comes closer than 14 units except the ones above. ﬁ and ﬂ are f beside i and l; f clears both by more than the o–o gap at every weight (56 and 45 against 42 at Black). They are encoded only so that text which already holds U+FB01 and U+FB02 shows them; no feature makes them, since f needs no ligature to clear i and l. Each is as wide as f and i or f and l set side by side and takes f's class kerning on its left.
- `maths.py`: ∆ and ◊ keep 41% and 43% of their Regular insides at Black, close to the 0 (46%). ∏ ∑ ∫ span the bracket height, −148 to 750, so they line up inside parentheses.

The default features change only forms, never characters: `ccmp`, `locl`, `mark`, `mkmk` and `kern` pick mark forms, local forms and positions, and `calt` raises punctuation between capitals and figures. The arrows and × sit in `ss03`, so typing -> or 2x3 gives exactly those characters unless the app asks for the set.

The marks over i and j (and I, J, Cyrillic і ј and Greek ι) are drawn at 96% of the width of the other marks: the circumflex is 240 units wide at Regular against 250 on ĉ, and the dieresis 271 against 278. They used to be narrowed to 200 (and 208), which rendered as a dot at 11 to 14 px and made ĵ look like j. The accent clearance kerning makes the room for them against T, V, W, Y, F and f, with 4 units more clearance over i and j than over other letters: the kerning is measured at the masters and interpolated between them, and at Medium the plain clearance left Tî and Tĵ 4 units short. So Tï now kerns +39 at Regular (+74 at Bold) and Tî +18 (+41), where the narrow marks needed none. At Black they sit 14 units higher, so hinting doesn't join the tilde of ĩ to its stem at 14 px.

Every file the build writes, the fourteen statics, the two variable fonts and all their WOFF2 copies, carries the UI names of ss01–ss06 (`featureNames`) and cv01–cv08 (`cvParameters`, with the character each one changes) in its name table; the cut statics keep them through the name pruning, and the tests read them back from every file.

The character variants with accented forms work like cv02: an accented letter becomes the variant base followed by its combining mark, placed by `mark` from anchors measured on the variant (`MARK_TWINS` in `alternates.py`). ť and ŧ have their own glyphs under cv08, since the caron beside the stem and the bar need the spurless stem's position; a typed t with a combining caron joins into ť's variant too. ǥ keeps its single-storey form under cv06.

## Not done

- The variable fonts are unhinted, so they render softer than the statics on Windows at small sizes.
- No weights outside 300 to 900.
- No scripts beyond Latin, Greek and Cyrillic.
- Greek capitals that carry a breathing get no kerning on their left, since the mark stands at cap height beside them and would run into a T or Γ bar; ΤἈ keeps its full gap. Capitals with the iota adscript get no kerning on their right. U+1FEF and U+1FFD use the Latin ` and ´, as Unicode equates them, so they don't match the steeper Greek tonos.
- Ұ, Ҭ, the tailed Cyrillic letters and the hooked and barred ones kern toward the letter they are built on (Ү, Т, К, Х, Ж, Н, Ч, and Й for Ҋ): each pair takes that letter's kerned gap, but never less than the floor, so where a bar or tail sticks out (Ұ. at Black) the pair stays looser.
- У and Ж kern in the Latin Y, K and X classes. Г, Ґ, Ѓ and Greek Γ have a class of their own (`Ge` in `features.py`): their bar runs further right than half of T's, so they take the T rows with more room under the bar for round letters, the period and the hyphen. Letters with no Latin model get their own rows: л, љ, д and з, whose tops start inside their feet, tuck under Г, Т, У, у and г; т and ъ, whose bar overhangs on the left, after А, К and Ь; Ф and Φ on both sides, since their bowl is wider than O's at the middle and narrower at the top; Ь, Ъ, Љ and Њ, open above the bowl, before Т, У and т; Ψ before the period; ζ and ξ, open under their top stroke, before round letters; and λ, whose stroke leaves its foot at the middle, after round letters, γ and ν. в, г, м, ц, ч, щ, ь, њ, ґ, қ, ҷ, к and ĸ kern like n on their left side, and ю like p (a stem on the left, a bowl on the right). Д and Л have flat tops at cap height, so ТД and УЛ space like ТН; only Г's longer bar reaches over them (ГЛ, ГД). Я keeps only the minimum-gap floor, since its leg runs out to the left at the baseline. Where one of these rows brings ink closer than the floor in any master, that pair is raised for the letter alone, as in the Latin classes.
- Italic kerns that `features.py` sets for single pairs do not carry across a combining mark; only the class kerns do.
- At Black the bottom of the T and Г bars lies on the x-height, so before v, w, x and y (and у, ў, ӯ) the kerning stops where the corners come about 16 units apart: Ty kerns −17 and Tv −18 at Black against −41 at Regular, and looks looser. Г, Ґ and Γ stop earlier (Гу −32 at Black against −76 at Regular), since Ґ comes closer to y in the Black Italic. A raised bar at Black would let them close up.
- At Light the bar of ₭ cannot cover both the crotch of K's leg and the joint of its arm with the stem without being heavier than the stem, so a small triangle of counter stays open under the bar; from Regular up the bar covers both.
- Abkhaz Ҩ ҩ, Ԩ ԩ (en with left hook) and Ӊ's distinction from Ӈ: Ӊ ӊ keep the shallow tail they had before Ӈ ӈ arrived, so the two differ only in depth, the hook of Ӈ reaching the descender.
